9.1 · Earth's Internal Structure

From Core to Crust

Core
Iron and nickel. Solid inner core; liquid outer core. Its rotation generates Earth's magnetic field.
Mantle
84% of Earth's volume; semi-molten rock that flows slowly over geologic time.
Crust
Thin, brittle outermost layer. The solid surface we live on.
Magnetosphere
The magnetic field shields Earth from charged solar particles and guides animal migration.
Litho- / Asthenosphere
Rigid lithosphere (crust + upper mantle, ~60–90 mi) rides on the pliable asthenosphere.

9.1 · Migration, Magnetic Fields & Monarch Butterflies

Navigating by Earth's Magnetic Field

  • As Earth rotates, its liquid outer core generates a magnetic field, the same field that moves a compass needle and helps animals find their way.
  • Monarch butterflies use magnetic sensors in their antennae to migrate from Canada and the US to Mexico each fall, returning north in spring and summer.
  • Many other animals navigate the same way: rainbow trout, the Arctic tern, and sea turtles all rely on the magnetic field.

9.2 · How Does Earth Recycle and Renew Its Surface?

The Rock Cycle

Igneous Rock
Formed by cooling magma. Hawaii's lava flows create new land as they solidify.
Weathering & Erosion
Wind, water, ice, and gravity break down and transport rock particles. Human farming accelerated the 1930s Dust Bowl.
Sedimentary Rock
Eroded particles (sediment) accumulate in layers and are lithified under pressure into solid rock.
Metamorphic Rock
Existing rock transformed by intense pressure and heat into a new rock type.
© 2023 W. W. Norton & Company, Inc.
9.3 · Why Are Minerals Important, and How Do We Get Them?

Rare Earth Elements

  • Minerals from Earth's crust are essential for electronics, solar panels, wind turbines, and electric vehicles. Most rare earth metals come from China.
  • Global dependence: rare earth “supply” is geographically concentrated, creating strategic vulnerabilities.
  • Environmental cost: produces radioactive mill tailings, toxic ponds, and waste sludge (causing crop death and community illness).
  • Implication: the 'clean energy' transition requires mining that carries its own environmental justice concerns.
  • Sustainability strategy: recycle and reuse rare earth minerals from consumer electronics to reduce extraction demand.

9.3 · Case Study: The Costs of Rare Earth Mining in Baotou

The Hidden Cost of Clean Tech

  • Baotou is a leading producer of the rare earth metals used in wind turbines and electric cars.
  • Refining these metals generates waste sludge (“mill tailings”) that contains hazardous and radioactive elements.
  • The waste sits in large toxic ponds; nearby crops have died and local livestock and residents have become sick.
  • Takeaway: the clean-energy transition carries its own environmental-justice costs at the point of extraction.

9.4 · What Shapes Earth's Surface?

Landscapes, Landforms & Watersheds

  • A landscape is built from landforms (its surface features) and topography (the shape of the land).
  • A watershed is the area of land draining to a point along a river (the Mississippi drains 31 states and 2 Canadian provinces).
  • Floodplains slow rivers and let sediment settle; a delta is a landform formed by the deposition of sediments at a river's mouth.

9.4 · Processes That Shape the Land

Moving Sediment, Ice & Water

Sediment & Headwaters
Sediment originates in elevated headwaters and is carried by river networks to depositional environments (floodplains, deltas, and estuaries).
Landslides
Large mass movements reshape landscapes quickly. Forestry and excavation can trigger them.
Glaciers
A glacier is a mass of ice that flows over land and persists year-round, sculpting valleys as it moves.
Overland Flow & Runoff
When soil is saturated or rain falls too fast, water runs off. Impervious paved surfaces send even more water as runoff.

9.5 · What Is Soil, and Where Does It Come From?

Soil Formation & Structure

Soil: a complex mixture of weathered rock, mineral particles, decaying organic matter, water, air, and living organisms. Formed where lithosphere meets biosphere.

O Horizon (Organic)
Surface layer of decomposing plant/animal matter; topmost and nutrient-rich.
A Horizon (Topsoil)
Complex mix of minerals and organic matter. Most biologically active layer.
B Horizon (Subsoil)
Mineral matter leached from above. Less organic content.
C Horizon (Parent Material)
Weathered bedrock or sediment. Foundation for soil development above.

9.7 · How Can Humans Repair and Sustain the Land?

Soil Erosion

Agriculture & Erosion
Soil erosion is soil loss caused by weathering, wind, and water removing soil. Tillage exposes soil to wind and water erosion. Overgrazing, over-irrigation, and excess chemicals further degrade soil. Plow-based farming on Roman upland slopes ultimately reached bedrock.

9.7 · How Can Humans Repair and Sustain the Land?

Restoration

Sustainable Farming
Terracing, cover crops, hedgerows, crop rotation, and organic fertilizer reduce erosion and build fertility.
Grazing Management
Rotating livestock allows degraded soils to recover. Sustainable grazing maintains ground cover.
Mine Reclamation
Importing topsoil, planting trees, and sustainable grazing can rehabilitate mine sites. The Eden Project (Cornwall, England) transformed clay mines into the world's largest greenhouse.

9.7 · Mining

Mining

  • Mining practices are not considered to be sustainable but abandoned mine sites can be reclaimed and rehabilitated to improve their ecological value.
  • Rehabilitative measures include importing topsoil, planting trees, and sustainable livestock grazing.
  • Globally, mines in Mongolia, the European Union, and China have been successfully rehabilitated.

9.7 · Forestry, Urbanization, and Sustainable Land Use

Forestry, Urbanization, and Sustainable Land Use

Deforestation
Humans deforest 13 million hectares per year (larger than Pennsylvania). Deforestation causes land degradation. Deforestation is incredibly consequential and directly impacts all life-support systems.
Urbanization
Land-use change: converting forests into urban areas. Cities reclaim brownfields (contaminated industrial sites) for new use. San Antonio's River Walk restored a waterfront as a community amenity.
Green Urban Design
Rain gardens, permeable surfaces, and vegetation-based stormwater management reduce runoff and restore infiltration.
Values & Land Use
Sustainable urban planning integrates economic and political choiceshose values shape how land is used?

9.8 · What Can I Do?

Sustaining the Land

  • Recycle and conserve rare earth mineral. Avoid unnecessary electronics upgrades; recycle old devices properly.
  • Know how your food affects soil health and choose sustainably grown products. Healthy soil supports plant growth and nutrient cycling.
  • Get informed about development decisions in your area by attending city planning meetings.
  • Be prepared for natural hazards. Learn about local risks from earthquakes, landslides, and flooding.
